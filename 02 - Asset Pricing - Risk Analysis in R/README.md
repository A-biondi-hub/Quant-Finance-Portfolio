# Asset Pricing and Risk Analysis in R

Implementation from scratch, in R, of the main building blocks of quantitative asset pricing and financial risk management: yield-curve calibration, option pricing, Greeks, PCA on rates, VaR/ES, structural and copula-based credit risk, and Basel IRB capital for a retail loan portfolio.

**Full report (code, output, plots and commentary): [`asset_pricing_risk_analysis.md`](asset_pricing_risk_analysis.md)**

## Content

| Part | Topic | Methods |
|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Yield curve and bond pricing | Svensson spot/forward curves, bond pricing, yield to maturity, calibration of the six parameters on market prices (Nelder-Mead) |
| 2 | Option pricing | Monte Carlo (Euler and exact distribution), Cox-Ross-Rubinstein binomial tree, Black-Scholes-Merton, comparison of prices and computing times |
| 3 | Greeks and hedging | Delta, Gamma and Vega of a long strangle, finite-difference check, delta-hedge |
| 4 | PCA on interest rates | Eigen-decomposition of the covariance of rate changes, factor loadings, 99% VaR with two factors vs full covariance |
| 5 | VaR and Expected Shortfall | Historical simulation vs Gaussian parametric model on a four-asset portfolio |
| 6 | Merton model | Equity as a call on the assets, risky debt, credit spreads by issuer |
| 7 | Copulas | Gaussian vs Student's t copula, marginal and joint default probabilities |
| 8 | IRB for a retail portfolio | PD, LGD and EAD calibration on peer-to-peer loan data, binomial EL/UL, Basel retail correlation and Vasicek capital, logit scoring model, one-factor Monte Carlo credit portfolio model |

## Highlights

- **Option pricing**: with 20,000 scenarios the Monte Carlo prices differ from the closed formula by an amount compatible with the standard error (about 0.06), while the binomial tree shows a small, systematic discretisation error.
- **PCA on rates**: the first two components explain 98.9% of the variance of the rate changes; the two-factor VaR underestimates the full-covariance VaR.
- **Copulas**: with the same marginals (PD = 3%) and correlation (0.25), the t copula gives a joint default probability about 2.1 times higher than the Gaussian one.
- **Retail credit portfolio**: ignoring correlation gives an unexpected loss that is tens of times smaller than the capital implied by the Basel/Vasicek framework, which shows how strongly the correlation assumption drives the result.

## How to run

1. Put the input CSV files in the `data/` folder (see [`data/README.md`](data/README.md)).
2. Install the packages:

   ```r
   install.packages(c("mvtnorm", "moments", "tidyverse", "rmarkdown"))
   ```

3. From this folder, render the report:

   ```r
   rmarkdown::render("asset_pricing_risk_analysis.Rmd")
   ```

Every random simulation has its own `set.seed()`, so the results are reproducible.

## Tools
